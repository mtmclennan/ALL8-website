# ALL8 Webworks: Contractor Positioning, SEO & Conversion Copy Overhaul

## Objective

Audit and improve the homepage, pricing page, and About page of ALL8 Webworks.

Website: https://all8webworks.com/

Repository: `mtmclennan/ALL8-website`

This is a focused marketing, SEO, and conversion-copy improvement project, **not a visual redesign or website rebuild**.

The website has been developed extensively, but some messaging has become overly technical, complicated, repetitive, or focused on explaining the underlying systems rather than the results customers want.

Our goal is to make ALL8 immediately understandable and appealing to **contractors and trades business owners**.

## 1. Brand positioning

ALL8 Webworks specializes in websites, Google visibility, lead capture, and follow-up systems for contractors.

### Primary target customers

Owner-operated and small-to-midsize contracting businesses, including:

- Excavation and sitework contractors
- Plumbers
- HVAC contractors
- Electricians
- Landscapers and property maintenance businesses
- Roofers
- Other residential and commercial service contractors

These are generally hands-on business owners who manage crews, equipment, jobs, customers, and estimates.

They understand the operational side of their businesses but may not understand or care about technical marketing terminology.

### What these customers want

- More qualified phone calls
- More estimate and quote requests
- More profitable jobs
- Better visibility on Google and Google Maps
- A professional website that builds trust
- Fewer missed inquiries
- Better follow-up on quotes and potential customers
- Less time dealing with marketing and administrative work

The messaging should reflect these problems and outcomes.

### Core positioning

**ALL8 helps contractors get found, get contacted, follow up, and win more work.**

ALL8 is not simply a website design agency.

It connects high-performance websites, local search visibility, inquiry handling, and practical automation into a system that supports business growth.

However, the customer should understand the business outcome before being introduced to the technology.

### Writing principles

- Use plain English familiar to contractors.
- Speak to the owner, not a marketing department.
- Focus on business outcomes before features.
- Be confident and professional, without sounding overly promotional.
- Avoid exaggerated guarantees or unsupported performance claims.
- Reduce jargon, buzzwords and unnecessary explanation.
- Keep paragraphs short and mobile-friendly.
- Explain technical services only when necessary.
- Preserve useful SEO terminology in natural context.
- Make ALL8 sound like a capable business partner who understands contracting operations.

Avoid leading with phrases such as:

- Lead pipeline architecture
- Attribution
- Conversion infrastructure
- Revenue optimization
- Omnichannel engagement
- Lead journey orchestration
- Integrated growth ecosystem

Prefer customer-facing language such as:

- Get more calls from Google
- Turn website visitors into quote requests
- Know where your inquiries are coming from
- Follow up on missed calls and estimates
- Stop losing jobs because nobody followed up

Do not eliminate all technical terms where they serve real search intent or help buyers understand a specific service.

## 3. Homepage improvements

The homepage should immediately answer:

- What does ALL8 do?
- Who is it for?
- How does it help my contracting business?
- Why should I trust ALL8?
- What should I do next?

### Hero positioning

Recommended direction:

**Headline:**
More Calls. More Quote Requests. Fewer Jobs Slipping Through.

**Supporting copy:**
Websites, Google visibility, and follow-up systems built for contractors. We help you get found, turn visitors into real inquiries, and stay on top of opportunities before they disappear.

**Primary CTA:**
Get My Free Website & Lead Review

**Secondary CTA:**
See Our Work

This is recommended positioning, not mandatory exact copy. Review the existing implementation and refine it where appropriate.

The primary headline should be concise and outcome-focused. Preserve the relevant website and contractor SEO terminology through the visible supporting copy, page title, section headings and body content.

### Homepage content structure

Evaluate the current structure against this proposed journey:

1. Hero: outcomes and primary CTA
2. Common contractor problems: not getting found, missed calls, website inquiries, estimates without follow-up
3. How ALL8 helps: website, Google visibility, inquiry capture and follow-up
4. Real results: credible case study and supporting proof
5. Why ALL8: practical contractor experience and technical expertise
6. Simple process: how getting started works
7. Relevant FAQ
8. Final CTA

Do not automatically delete useful sections. Consolidate repeated messaging and remove explanations that don't help customers make a decision.

Keep the missed-call calculator accessible and consider a contextual link where it genuinely supports the buying journey.

Use one clear primary CTA consistently.

### SEO objectives

Optimize naturally for relevant commercial searches such as:

- Websites for contractors
- Contractor website design
- Contractor SEO
- Local SEO for contractors
- Contractor lead generation
- Websites for service businesses

Do not stuff all keywords into one heading or repeat them unnaturally.

Review the homepage SEO title, meta description, H1, H2 structure and internal links. Balance search intent with readability and conversion.

## 4. Pricing page improvements

The pricing page should be simple, transparent, and easy to understand.

### Remove currency commentary

Remove public marketing references explaining:

- Canadian dollar pricing
- U.S. dollar pricing
- The same numerical pricing in both countries
- Exchange-rate differences
- Applicable currency explanations
- Currency comparison FAQ entries

Display plan prices simply as:

`$299/mo`

And setup prices as:

`$1,995 setup`

These are examples of existing amounts, not instructions to change plan pricing. Verify all current amounts in the repository.

Do not change existing prices, taxes, billing logic, payment currency configuration, or stored pricing data.

Currency must still be disclosed clearly wherever a customer receives a formal quote, checkout, invoice, or binding agreement. Do not introduce ambiguous or incorrect billing disclosures.

### Remove public hourly rates

Remove the advertised `$110/hour` additional-work rate and other public hourly-rate references used for pricing.

Replace with wording such as:

"Additional work is quoted and approved before it begins."

Update any related FAQ answers and repeated pricing copy.

Do not change the actual internal cost model.

### Preserve important purchasing information

Keep clearly visible:

- Monthly fees
- Setup fees
- Minimum commitments
- What's included
- What's not included
- How additional work is approved
- What happens when a customer needs something outside a plan

Distinguish clearly between a free review with no obligation and any plan that has a minimum contract term.

Avoid vague statements such as "no commitment" where they could misrepresent a paid plan.

### Improve pricing-page conversion

Review the current plan descriptions.

Each plan should clearly explain:

- Who it is for
- What problem it solves
- What the customer receives
- When to choose it over another plan

Avoid turning the pricing page into a technical specification sheet.

The progression between Managed Website, Lead System, and Growth System should be obvious.

Preserve the underlying scope and plan differences. Don't invent inclusions or simplify language in ways that create new contractual promises.

### Pricing SEO

Preserve clear terms such as contractor website pricing, managed websites, contractor lead systems and local SEO services where relevant.

Review metadata, FAQ content and structured data for consistency with the visible pricing.

## 5. About page improvements

Retarget the About page toward contractors and trades business owners.

Avoid broad positioning that prominently lists recruiting, general professional services, or unrelated industries.

These businesses may still be potential clients, but they are not our primary marketing audience.

### Founder story

ALL8's founder has a background in the trades and contracting industry.

Relevant experience includes:

- Licensed automotive service technician (310S)
- Heavy equipment maintenance and repair
- Excavation and construction operations
- Estimating and construction bids
- Contractor websites and marketing
- Business development
- Software development and practical automation

This is a meaningful differentiator.

Rather than claiming to understand contractors because of market research, explain that ALL8 was built by someone who understands the operational realities of contracting businesses.

Don't exaggerate professional experience, imply ownership of businesses not owned, or invent projects or results.

### Core About-page message

Suggested direction:

"Built by someone who understands the work behind the website."

Explain that the founder understands the challenge of keeping equipment working, crews busy, estimates moving and customers coming in.

Connect that background to why ALL8 focuses on practical websites and systems that actually help contractors run and grow their businesses.

Keep the story authentic, concise, professional and personal.

### Trust and proof

Use existing verified project results, testimonials and founder information.

Do not invent client names, testimonials, revenue increases, job counts or conversion metrics.

If the existing case study measures search visibility or website clicks, describe those exact outcomes. Do not turn them into unverified increases in booked jobs.

Preserve existing founder photography and credible business details.

## 6. Calls-to-action and terminology

Audit the three pages for inconsistent CTA wording.

Recommend a coherent primary CTA such as:

**Get My Free Website & Lead Review**

Secondary CTAs can use:

- See Our Work
- Explore Services
- View Pricing
- Text Us

Use action labels that tell visitors what will happen.

If changing a CTA label could imply a different deliverable than the existing lead-review process, adjust the supporting description instead of changing the actual service promise.

Maintain the existing form, modal, tracking, HubSpot integration and event semantics.

Review the phrases "lead leak," "lead path," "lead system," and similar internal terminology. Keep them only where they add clarity or distinguish an actual product.

Ensure the four-stage/five-stage framework terminology is consistent across the pages.

- Do not introduce redirects unnecessarily.
- Use one meaningful H1 per page.

- Improve titles and descriptions without keyword stuffing.

- Maintain meaningful internal links to existing services and case studies.
- Keep server-rendered content crawlable.

- Maintain existing performance improvements.
- Do not add unnecessary client-side JavaScript.
- Do not introduce deceptive urgency, guarantees or fabricated social proof.

We will build those separately under `/industries/[slug]`, beginning with excavation and plumbing.

The current work should establish the correct positioning for those future pages.

## Success criteria

Within five seconds of landing on the homepage, a contractor should understand:

**ALL8 helps contractors get more opportunities, turn those opportunities into calls and quote requests, and follow up so fewer jobs get missed.**

The visitor should feel that ALL8 understands contracting businesses, not just websites and marketing software.

The pricing should be easy to compare without unnecessary explanations or objections.

The About page should establish authentic credibility through relevant experience.

The entire experience should be simpler, more trustworthy, more search-relevant, and more likely to turn the right visitors into qualified inquiries.
